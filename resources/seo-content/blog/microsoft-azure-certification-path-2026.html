Microsoft has changed a lot of its Azure certifications during 2026. Some exams you may have read about have been retired, and new ones have taken their place. This guide walks through the Azure certification path as it stands now, from fundamentals to associate to expert, and points out which older advice is out of date.

Facts come from Microsoft Learn and Microsoft's own credentials announcement, and were checked on November 5, 2026. Certification details change, so confirm on the official pages before you book anything.

The path in one picture

  1. Fundamentals: Azure Fundamentals (AZ-900).
  2. Associate: Azure Administrator Associate (AZ-104), plus role-specific associate exams such as networking (AZ-700) and security (SC-500).
  3. Expert: Azure Solutions Architect Expert (AZ-305) and DevOps Engineer Expert (AZ-400).

The most important rule to know is that the expert certifications have prerequisites. They are not just a harder exam you can book on its own.

Step 1: Azure Fundamentals (AZ-900)

Azure Fundamentals is rated Beginner on Microsoft Learn and is described as a common starting point in a journey towards a career in Azure. It covers cloud concepts, core Azure services, and Azure management and governance. Microsoft says Fundamentals certifications do not expire, so this one is yours for good once you earn it.

You do not need hands-on experience, which makes it a good first step for people in infrastructure, database and software roles who are moving to Azure. If you are still deciding whether to start here or go straight to administration, read AZ-900 vs AZ-104: which should you take first?

Step 2: Azure Administrator Associate (AZ-104)

The Azure Administrator Associate certification is rated Intermediate. Microsoft expects you to have experience implementing, managing and monitoring an Azure environment, including virtual networks, storage, compute, identity, security and governance, and to be familiar with PowerShell, the Azure CLI, the Azure portal, ARM templates or Bicep, and Microsoft Entra ID. It must be renewed every 12 months, which is free and done online.

This certification matters beyond its own value, because both expert certifications build on it.

Step 3: the expert certifications

Azure Solutions Architect Expert (AZ-305)

This certification is for people who design cloud and hybrid solutions on Azure, advise stakeholders and translate business requirements into designs. To earn it you pass AZ-305, and Microsoft's certification page lists the Azure Administrator Associate certification as the prerequisite. In practice, that means AZ-104 comes before AZ-305 on the path. Microsoft expects advanced experience across networking, virtualization, identity, security, business continuity, disaster recovery, data platforms and governance, along with experience in Azure administration, development and DevOps processes.

DevOps Engineer Expert (AZ-400)

The DevOps Engineer Expert certification requires AZ-400 and at least one of two associate certifications: Azure Administrator Associate or Azure Developer Associate. This is where 2026 has changed things: Microsoft's Azure Developer Associate page now states that the certification and its renewal assessment are retired. Check the DevOps Engineer Expert page for the current list before you plan around it. For most people the Azure Administrator Associate route is the one that is still available.

Which path should you follow?

  • I want to run Azure: AZ-900 (optional) then AZ-104. Add AZ-700 if networking is your area.
  • I want to design Azure solutions: AZ-104 first, then AZ-305. The AZ-305 guide shows the four design domains.
  • I want to work in DevOps: AZ-104, then AZ-400, subject to the current prerequisites on Microsoft's page.
  • I want to secure Azure: look at SC-500, which Microsoft recommends in place of the retired AZ-500, and compare it with the other security and identity exams.
  • I want to build AI applications on Azure: look at AI-103 and AI-200 in the data and AI track. These are new, so read the study guide dates.

A sensible timeline

If you start from zero, a realistic first year looks like this. Spend the first month on AZ-900. Spend the next two or three months on AZ-104, with hands-on practice every week. Then decide whether your job calls for design (AZ-305), DevOps (AZ-400), networking (AZ-700) or security. These are our suggestions, not Microsoft requirements, and the right pace depends on how much of the material you use at work.

Keep your certifications current

Associate and expert certifications expire after a year unless you renew them. Renewal is free and online, in a six-month window before the expiry date. Our post on how Microsoft certification renewal works covers the details.

Mistakes to avoid when planning your path

  • Following a path from an old blog post. AZ-204 and AZ-500 appear in many guides and have both been retired in 2026. Check the exam page before you start.
  • Booking an expert exam before the prerequisite. The expert certifications list prerequisite certifications. Passing the expert exam alone does not complete the certification.
  • Collecting fundamentals exams for the sake of it. One fundamentals exam in your area is useful. Five of them rarely help your career as much as one associate certification.
  • Ignoring renewal. Every associate and expert certification expires after a year unless you renew it.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need AZ-900 to start the Azure path?

No. It is a common starting point, not a requirement. If you already work with Azure, you can begin with AZ-104.

Which expert certification should I choose?

Choose by role. If you design solutions, the Solutions Architect Expert fits. If you build delivery pipelines and practices, the DevOps Engineer Expert fits. Both require an associate certification first.

How long does the whole path take?

It depends on your experience, so we do not give a single number. As a guide, our study plans allow about two weeks for AZ-900 and three for AZ-104, and each expert exam needs more preparation and real project experience.
