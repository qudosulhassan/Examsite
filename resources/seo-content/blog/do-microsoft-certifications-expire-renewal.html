Yes, most Microsoft certifications expire, but keeping them current is free and does not mean sitting the original exam again. Microsoft's associate, expert and specialty certifications expire after a year unless you renew them with a short online assessment. Fundamentals certifications do not expire at all. This guide explains exactly how renewal works, when you can do it, and how to avoid losing a certification by accident.

Everything here comes from Microsoft Learn's certification renewal page and study guides, checked on November 5, 2026. Microsoft can change its renewal rules, so confirm on the official page and in your Microsoft Learn profile.

Which certifications expire?

  • Fundamentals certifications do not expire. Microsoft states this on its renewal page. If you earn Azure Fundamentals through AZ-900, or Security, Compliance, and Identity Fundamentals through SC-900, you keep it.
  • Associate, expert and specialty certifications expire annually. Microsoft's study guides say this directly: these certifications expire annually, and you renew by passing a free online assessment on Microsoft Learn. On the certification pages, the renewal frequency for the Azure Administrator Associate and Security Operations Analyst Associate is listed as 12 months.

If you hold a certification that is being retired, nothing changes for what you already earned: Microsoft says that a certification you have already earned remains valid until it expires.

How renewal works, step by step

  1. Connect your profiles. Connect your Microsoft Learn profile with your certification profile. Once they are connected you can see your certifications, their expiry dates and a renew button when you are eligible.
  2. Wait for your window. You can take the renewal assessment at any time during a six-month eligibility window before the certification expires. Microsoft says you will receive an email reminding you to renew once you are eligible.
  3. Refresh your skills. The renewal page for each certification collects free, self-paced learning modules that focus on recent changes.
  4. Pass the assessment. The assessment is online, free, unproctored and open book. You can take it as many times as you need, as long as you pass before the certification expires.

When you pass, your certification is extended one year from its expiration date. That detail matters: renewing early does not shorten your next cycle, because the extra year is counted from the old expiry date, not from the day you pass.

Because the assessment concentrates on what has changed recently, the best preparation is not to re-study the whole exam. Read what Microsoft has changed since you passed, mostly in the updated study guide, and use the free modules on your renewal page.

A simple renewal routine

  • Put the expiry date in your calendar the day you pass, and add a second reminder at the start of the six-month window.
  • Renew early in the window. The assessment can be repeated, but a failed attempt in the last week leaves little time to prepare. Since the extension runs from the old expiry date, you lose nothing by doing it early.
  • Check your email and profile address. The reminder goes to the address on your profile. A reminder in an unused mailbox is a reminder you never see.
  • Re-read the study guide. Check the date at the top of the outline against your last pass. If Microsoft has updated it, note which domains changed.

Common questions

Do I have to take the exam again?

No. Renewal is a shorter assessment, not the original exam.

Does renewal cost anything?

No. Microsoft says there is no cost to renew, as long as you pass before the certification expires.

Can I use notes or the documentation?

Yes. The assessments are open book and unproctored. That does not make them trivial, since the questions are written to test whether you know what changed.

What if my certification expires?

The renewal page tells you to pass the assessment before your certification expires. If a certification has already expired, check the certification renewal FAQ and your Microsoft Learn profile for what your options are. We cannot tell you what Microsoft will allow in your case.

Does a retired exam affect renewal?

Microsoft says a certification you have already earned stays valid until it expires. Retirement affects whether you can earn a certification through that exam, so check the individual certification page. For example, Microsoft's page for the Azure Developer Associate states that the certification and its renewal assessment are retired.

Renewal and your career plan

Renewal is the reason to treat certifications as something you maintain, not something you collect. A role-based certification that is current tells an employer that you have kept up with a platform that changes every few months. An expired one tells them only that you passed once.

If you are choosing between exams, expiry is one factor. Fundamentals certifications, such as AZ-900, SC-900, PL-900 and DP-900, are one-time efforts. Role-based certifications such as AZ-104 or SC-200 need a short yearly renewal, which is also a prompt to look at what has changed in your field.

A worked example

This example is illustrative, so use your own dates. Say you passed an associate exam on March 15, 2026. The certification would then expire a year later, on March 15, 2027. Based on Microsoft's six-month window, you could take the renewal assessment at any time from about September 2026 until March 2027. If you pass in October 2026, the certification is extended one year from the old expiry date, so it would then run until March 2028.

Notice what that means in practice: you cannot gain extra time by waiting, and you lose nothing by renewing as soon as the window opens. Check the exact dates in your own Microsoft Learn profile, because they depend on when your certification was earned or last renewed.

Renewing versus earning a new certification

Renewing keeps an existing certification current. It does not add a new one. If your goal is a new role or a new level, you still need the new certification's exam. A useful pattern is to renew each year and use the study time to notice where your field has moved, then decide what to learn next. A short checklist

  • Link your Microsoft Learn profile and your certification profile.
  • Write down the expiry date of each certification you hold.
  • Set a reminder for the start of your six-month window.
  • Make sure the email address on your profile is one you read.
  • Skim the current study guide to see what changed since you passed.
  • Take the assessment early, and retake it if you need to, before the expiry date.
